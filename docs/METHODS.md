# Drone Fly methods and limitations

Protocol version: `drone-fly-camera-v1`. Initial experiment: September 13, 2026. This document describes the shipped implementation, not biological flight or physical-drone validation.

## Aircraft and environment

The MuJoCo XML is generated by `physics.model_xml`. A free joint gives the generic 0.32 kg aircraft six degrees of freedom. Four site actuators apply positive body-z thrust and alternating yaw reaction torque, with arms at ±0.12 m. Principal moments are 0.0023, 0.0023 and 0.004 kg m². Motor thrust is limited to 0–2.4 N. These are authored generic parameters, not manufacturer identification data.

MuJoCo integrates at 240 Hz. Every 20 Hz navigation decision advances 12 physics steps. A velocity-error controller produces a desired acceleration; gravity compensation determines desired thrust direction. A geometric attitude-error controller produces torque. A four-motor mixing matrix maps total thrust and torque to individual rotor forces. The loop never teleports the aircraft or writes its pose during a flight step. No detailed propeller airflow, sensor noise, battery model, aerodynamic drag or motor-lag identification is claimed.

World x runs along the hangar, y left, z up. A flight starts airborne at (0, 0, 1.7). Five gates are spaced along x, at 5 + 5.5i metres. Gate openings are 2.0 m wide and 1.8 m high. Course seeds select their lateral and vertical offsets. Slalom widens the lateral distribution. Static pillars flank the corridor; gate frames are the main collision obstacles. Crosswind adds a 0.18 sin(1.7t) N lateral force; the interactive wind setting adds another bounded force.

Passing is assessed at the interpolated forward crossing of each gate plane with a 0.16 m clearance margin. A missed opening increments misses. Actual MuJoCo body contacts terminate the flight. Passing all five without a miss completes it. Other terminal conditions are missed course, bounds violation, or 45 s timeout. Gate scoring is privileged environment logic; it is not exposed to the neural input.

The browser converts world axes to Three.js axes and interpolates display poses. It scales the visible drone mesh 1.5× for legibility; physics remains unchanged. Browser low walls are a display cutaway of the full collision walls. This visualization is not a second physics engine.

## Camera and observations

MuJoCo renders a 160×120 RGB image through a forward-facing 75° vertical-FOV camera mechanically stabilized along world x. The camera sits 0.20 m forward and 0.025 m above the aircraft origin. The active gate is orange; later/passed gates are neutral. This is a deliberately marked target task, not general obstacle perception.

A pixel is a target when r > 65, r > 1.7g and r > 1.7b. At least five pixels are needed for a detection. The bounding box defines normalized horizontal and vertical center, width and height. The eight channels are:

1. Horizontal center, −1 left to +1 right in the camera.
2. Vertical center, −1 down to +1 up.
3. Box width / image width.
4. Box height / image height.
5. Visibility, 0 or 1.
6. Forward velocity / 2 m/s.
7. Lateral velocity / 2 m/s.
8. Vertical velocity / 1.5 m/s.

Channels are clipped to [−1,1]. Missing detections yield zero for channels 1–5. Velocity is an idealized inertial input. Gate coordinates, distances, obstacle lists and MuJoCo segmentation IDs do not enter these observations. Camera blackout and left masking act on RGB before feature extraction. The workbench displays that processed image with a bounding-box overlay.

An emitted frame contains the image/observation at `observedTime`, the activity that consumed it, and the physical state after the ensuing control interval at `time`. A paused observation refresh does not advance recurrent activity. JPEG compression is for transmission only; feature extraction uses the original RGB array.

## Circuit and readout

The pinned graph has 80 cells, 1,296 directed edges and 26,029 synaptic contacts. Its SHA-256 is `2424c9dd2e44534e600aeda1a9058039b1f22a4bd983284a27adc10b22130719`. Neuron positions, IDs and contact counts come from MaleCNS; the compact selection is credited to Fly Dino. Every output is reachable from a driven cell through nonzero-sign edges.

For each postsynaptic cell, normalize signed contact counts by the sum of absolute signed incoming counts. Acetylcholine contributes +1, GABA/glutamate −1, and unclear annotations zero. These are simplified modeling assumptions. Each of eight channels drives four selected visual cells, assigned using the upstream graph's channel indices. This assignment does not claim that the named cells naturally encode drone flight features.

At each decision, inject `drive[inputCell] = 1.5 × observation[channel]` and run three synchronous iterations:

```text
h_next = 0.3 h + 0.7 tanh(drive + 1.4 W h)
```

State persists across decisions and resets at episode/controller reset. Activity is dimensionless; neither spikes nor membrane voltage are simulated. Sixteen descending output states are scaled by 3, concatenated with a 96-unit fixed random tanh expansion and one bias. A 113×3 coefficient matrix predicts velocity, totaling 339 learned coefficients. The expansion uses NumPy generator seed 37, projection standard deviation 1.4 and bias standard deviation 0.35. No camera observation bypasses the selected output cells.

Velocity commands are bounded to forward [−0.5,2], lateral [−2,2], and vertical [−1.5,1.5] m/s. Heading stabilization remains conventional; this course is not arbitrary-yaw navigation.

## Training and controls

The explicit visual-servo teacher uses the same observation vector. When the gate is visible it commands:

```text
vx = 1.15 − 0.35 min(1, |horizontal| + |vertical|)
vy = clip(−4.8 horizontal, −1.7, 1.7)
vz = clip( 3.8 vertical,   −1.2, 1.2)
```

Without a visible gate it continues forward at 1 m/s with zero lateral/vertical command. Navigation performance is therefore an imitation test against a strong task-specific teacher, not an RL discovery.

Data collection uses seeds 100–123, cycling standard/slalom/gusts. The teacher's executed commands receive independent Gaussian perturbations with standard deviations (0.06,0.18,0.12) m/s, using seed 20260913; unperturbed teacher commands are the training targets. The exact same sequential observations and targets are supplied to all substrates. All are fitted by ridge regression, regularization 0.02, with the intercept unpenalized. No evaluation course selects weights or hyperparameters.

- **Measured:** original directed connectivity, contact weights and sign assumptions.
- **Rewired:** directed double-edge swaps, seed 19, preserving every node's in/out degree and each source edge's contact count. Same driven/output cells and recurrent/readout architecture. The rewiring routine does not add duplicate directed edges.
- **Random features:** conventional fixed 80-unit random projection of the eight inputs, with leaky tanh state. Sixteen indexed output units and the same 113-feature expansion/readout are used. It is a random-feature model, not a fully backpropagation-trained MLP.
- **Visual servo:** the handwritten teacher, independent of circuit activity. The circuit can still be monitored in this mode, but does not command the aircraft.

All learned substrates have 339 fitted parameters. Circuit silencing sets all activity to zero; output silencing clamps selected output cells. The artificial readout bias remains active, so silencing does not imply zero thrust or a safe stop. The flight stabilizer is never silenced.

## Evaluation and interpretation

Twelve course seeds 1000–1011 are disjoint from training seeds. Eight conditions use the same courses, a paired design. Per-course snapshots include status, passed/missed gates, collision identity, duration, trajectory length and motor-effort proxy. The latter integrates sum(thrust^1.5) and is not calibrated electrical energy; it is not displayed as joules.

`artifacts/benchmark.json` contains all 96 episodes and summaries. Wilson 95% intervals describe binomial completion uncertainty within a condition. They do not describe independent training-run variation or establish significance between paired conditions. Results must be interpreted with the small sample size and saturated intact-controller scores in mind.

The intact measured, rewired, random-feature and teacher policies all completed 12/12. The experiment supports successful simulated control through the circuit and sensory dependence. It provides **no evidence of a biological topology advantage**. More demanding tasks, multiple training seeds, controlled distribution shifts and paired statistical analysis would be needed to investigate that question.

The model fingerprint hashes the protocol version, graph hash and all fitted coefficient matrices. The benchmark stores that fingerprint, and browser frames record it. A graph/version mismatch or altered coefficients invalidates a checkpoint. Identical floating-point retraining across GPU drivers/renderers/platforms is not guaranteed: the pixel detector may receive slightly different rasterization near boundaries. Always evaluate the resulting checkpoint on the held-out set.

## Boundaries and future hardware

This is a local simulator with idealized sensing and marked gates. It does not demonstrate unmarked-obstacle avoidance, moving-obstacle handling, onboard embedded compute, biological flight fidelity, or sim-to-real transfer. A physical stage requires a selected airframe, camera and flight-controller interface, calibration, latency and dropout handling, and staged flight validation. The isolated navigation observation/velocity-command boundary is the extension point; no hardware connection is enabled in this release.
